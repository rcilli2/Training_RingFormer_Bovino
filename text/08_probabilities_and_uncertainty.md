# What does a probability vector tell us?

## Prediction and confidence

At each interval, the classifier produces six logits: unrestricted numerical scores. Softmax turns them into non-negative probabilities summing to one:

$$p_c=\frac{\exp(z_c)}{\sum_j\exp(z_j)}.$$

The predicted class is the one with the largest probability. Confidence is that largest probability. A confidence of 0.8 expresses the model's output; it does not establish that the prediction has an empirically verified 80% chance of being correct.

## Entropy describes how spread out the probabilities are

Predictive entropy is:

$$H[p]=-\sum_c p_c\log p_c.$$

It is close to zero when one class receives almost all probability. It reaches its maximum when all classes are equally likely. With six classes and natural logarithms, that maximum is $\log 6$, approximately 1.79 nats.

Confidence considers only the largest probability. Entropy uses the entire probability vector, so two predictions with equal confidence can have different entropy.

## Multiple predictions for the same interval

The UQ methods produce probability vectors $p_1,\ldots,p_M$. We first average them:

$$\bar p_c=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^{M}p_{m,c}.$$

The entropy of this average is total predictive uncertainty. The average of the individual entropies is expected conditional entropy:

$$H[\bar p]=-\sum_c\bar p_c\log\bar p_c,$$

$$\mathbb E[H[p_m]]=-\frac{1}{M}\sum_m\sum_c p_{m,c}\log p_{m,c}.$$

Their difference is the mutual-information score:

$$MI=H[\bar p]-\mathbb E[H[p_m]].$$

The expected entropy is used as an aleatoric proxy and MI as an epistemic proxy. MI measures disagreement in the sampled predictive distributions; it is not the variance of their entropy values.

## Same average, different disagreement

For a two-class illustration, suppose two members both predict $(0.5,0.5)$. The average is ambiguous, but there is no disagreement: total and expected entropy are both approximately 0.693 nats, so MI is zero.

Now suppose the members predict $(0.99,0.01)$ and $(0.01,0.99)$. The average is still $(0.5,0.5)$ and total entropy is unchanged. Each member is confident, however: expected entropy is approximately 0.056 and MI approximately 0.637.

This distinction is why we retain member probabilities rather than only their average.

## What the scores do not establish

All members can agree confidently on an incorrect class. Then both entropy and MI can be small despite a serious error. The decomposition describes the predictions generated by the chosen approximation, not every source of missing geological knowledge.

In particular, the five methods sample different sources of variability. Applying the same formula makes their scores mathematically comparable in form; it does not make their interpretation identical.
